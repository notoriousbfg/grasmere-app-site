Start Here

Welcome to Grasmere. This is a hands-on tour: each section says what a feature does and gives you something to try. The examples folder holds a small sample notebook to try things on, a first year on an allotment, and each section links to a guide with the details.

In Grasmere, choose Help ▸ Feature Tour to open this tour as a throwaway copy. It’s an ordinary folder of Markdown files, so edit, rename and break things as you like: each time you choose Feature Tour it starts afresh, and nothing you change here is kept. If you’re reading this on the web, download Grasmere and open the tour from its Help menu to follow along.

Writing

Plain files you own

Every note is a .md file in a folder you choose. There’s no database and no special syntax: links are ordinary Markdown links, so your notes read just as well in any other editor, on GitHub, or to an AI tool.

Try it: right-click “Start Here” in the contents and choose Show in Finder.

Bold, italic and lists

Bold is ⌘B and italic is ⌘I. Brackets and backticks come in pairs as you type them. Lists carry on when you press Return:

  • Return on a list line starts the next item
    • Tab nests an item, ⇧Tab brings it back out
  • Return on an empty item ends the list
  1. Numbered lists renumber themselves
  2. as you add and remove items

Try it: click at the end of “as you add and remove items” and press Return.

More in Writing and Formatting.

Pictures and tables

A line that’s just a picture link shows the picture beneath it, as on The Allotment. Paste a screenshot or drop an image file into a note, and it’s saved in the project’s images folder and linked.

Tables are drawn as tables until you click into them; then you edit the Markdown, and Tab moves between cells.

Try it: click a cell above and press Tab a few times. Then copy any picture, click on the empty line below, and paste.

Folding headings

Any heading can fold its section away. Folding only hides text on screen: the file never changes.

Try it: point at this heading and click the chevron in the left margin. View ▸ Fold All (⇧⌥⌘[) turns this note into a table of contents, and Unfold All (⇧⌥⌘]) opens it up again.

Focus mode

View ▸ Focus Mode (⇧⌘D) hides both sidebars, keeps the line you’re writing in the middle of the window, and fades everything but the paragraph you’re in.

Try it: press ⇧⌘D, type a sentence, and press ⇧⌘D again. More in Focus Mode and Split View.

Links

Links that stay correct

Rename or move a note, in Grasmere, in Finder or in a terminal, and every link to it is rewritten to match. Each update is listed in Edit ▸ Link Updates…, with Undo.

This paragraph links to Margaret and Crop Rotation.

Try it: double-click Margaret in the contents, call her “Margaret Hill”, press Return, and look at the link above.

Making links

Type [text]( and a picker opens: notes that match the text come first, with a preview of the highlighted one beside the list. Or select some words and press ⌘K. After a note’s name, type # to choose one of its headings, as in the shed.

Try it: on the empty line below, type [compost]( and press Return.

Previews

Rest the pointer on a link for a moment to see a preview: a note’s opening, a heading’s section, a quoted passage, a web page’s title, or a picture. Click the preview to open what it shows, or ⌘-click a link to follow it straight away.

Try it: point at Compost, then click the preview.

Links to passages

A link can point at a passage rather than a whole note. Select some text, choose Edit ▸ Copy Link to Selection (⌥⌘L), and paste the link into any note. Companion Planting quotes one of Margaret’s rules like this, and a small number in the margin of her note marks the quoted line.

Try it: open Margaret and point at the number beside the marigolds.

Broken links

A link to a note that isn’t there is shown muted, over a dotted underline, and its preview says so.

Try it: delete the backticks around [The Greenhouse](examples/places/Greenhouse.md): there’s no such note yet.

More on all of this in Links and Backlinks.

The margin

The margin on the right (⌃⌥⌘S shows or hides it) lists, for the open note, the notes that link to it, the notes it links to, the notes that mention it without linking, and, in a git repository, its history.

Try it: open Crop Rotation. The journal entry for 8th October names it without linking, so it’s listed under Mentioned in: click Link. More in The Margin.

Split view

Hold ⌥ and click a link, a note in the contents, a search result or a task to open it in a pane beside the page. View ▸ Close Side Pane (⌥⌘W) closes it.

Try it: ⌥-click Sowing Calendar.

Finding your way

Open Quickly and Search

Go ▸ Open Quickly (⌘P) opens a note by name. Go ▸ Search All Notes (⇧⌘F) searches the text of every note. ⌘F finds within the open note, and Go ▸ Back (⌘[) and Forward (⌘]) step through the notes you’ve opened, as in a browser.

Try it: press ⌘F, type “marigold”, and press Return a few times. Then press ⇧⌘F and search for “manure”. More in Find and Search.

The contents

The contents on the left (⌃⌘S shows or hides it) lists notes by their titles, with folders as small headings. Drag a note onto a folder to move it (the links follow), double-click to rename, and press ⌘1 to move through the list with the arrow keys.

A folder with an index.md is listed in the order its links reach its notes, rather than alphabetically. The top level follows the Order section of project.md, which is why this note comes first.

Try it: in the contents, open examples ▸ ideas and drag Compost above Crop Rotation. Then look at the ideas list again. More in The Contents.

The Index

View ▸ Index (⇧⌘L) shows every note as a card. ↑ and ↓ go through the deck; ← and → go along the links of the card in front. Return opens one.

Try it: press ⇧⌘L on this note, then → a few times. More in The Index.

Days and tasks

Today’s note

Go ▸ Today (⌘T) opens today’s note in a journal folder, making it if it isn’t there, titled like a diary. Previous Day (⌥⌘←) and Next Day (⌥⌘→) step between the days that have notes.

Try it: open Thursday 8th October and press ⌥⌘← to reach the Saturday before. Then press ⌘T. More in Daily Notes.

Tasks

Tasks are ordinary Markdown checkboxes. Click a box to tick it, or press ⌘↩ on its line:

  • ☐ Tick this box
  • ☑ Untick this one
  • ☐ Click this date to move it 📅 2026-10-16

A date at the end of a task gives it a due date. Click the date to pick another day from a calendar. Go ▸ Tasks (⇧⌘T) gathers every task in the project, by note or by date; Jobs for October has a few with dates.

Try it: press ⇧⌘T, choose By date, and tick something there. More in Tasks and Due Dates.

Word targets

Give the project, or any folder, a number of words to reach, by a date if you like. The bar beneath the page then shows how far you’ve got and the words written today.

Try it: choose Project ▸ Set Word Target…, set 1,000 words, then type a sentence here and pause. More in Writing Goals.

Capturing

The book in the menu bar, or ⌃⌥⌘N from any app, opens a small box: type a thought, press Return, and it’s added to today’s note in the project you choose. Captures go to your own projects, never to this tour.

Copy a web address in another app and come back to Grasmere: a card offers to clip the page into a Research folder or insert a link. In Safari, Finder and Photos, Share ▸ Grasmere sends a page, text, picture or file to one of your projects.

Try it: copy a web address in Safari, then click back into this window. More in Quick Capture and Sharing.

Thinking with your notes

Project ▸ Ask Your Notes… (⇧⌘A) answers a question from your notes alone, with numbered citations that open the passage each came from. It uses Apple’s on-device model unless you choose Claude or ChatGPT, with your own key, in Settings ▸ AI.

Try it: press ⇧⌘A and ask “What does Margaret say about tomatoes?” More in Ask Your Notes and AI.

Publishing

Right-click a folder and choose Publish to PDF… to typeset its notes as one PDF, each note a chapter. Project ▸ Build Website (⌥⌘B) turns the project into a website, a page for each note. This help is written in Grasmere and published that way.

Try it: right-click examples ▸ ideas, choose Publish to PDF…, and pick Book. Or press ⌥⌘B: the site is built inside this tour, so it’s thrown away too. More in Publishing.

Your own notes

File ▸ Open Folder… (⇧⌘O) opens any folder of Markdown files, or an empty one to start afresh. Your notes are left as they are: Grasmere adds a project.md at the top, for the project’s summary and order, and keeps its link index and settings to itself. The guides cover everything in more detail, and Support answers common questions.

That’s the tour. Close this window when you’re done: nothing here is kept.
