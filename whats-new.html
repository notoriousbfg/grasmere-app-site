What’s New

1.2 · 22 October 2026

  • Documents, not notes. Grasmere now calls what you write documents: in its menus, settings, messages and Shortcuts actions, on this site and in the Feature Tour. Daily notes are the journal (Settings ▸ General ▸ Journal), Go ▸ Today opens today’s entry, and Ask Your Notes is Ask Your Documents. Nothing on disk changes: your files, folders and settings are as they were.
  • Reopening takes you back to where you were. Grasmere opens at the document you were on, scrolled as you left it; and opening Grasmere again after closing its window brings the project back, not the start screen.
  • A runbook from New Document with Questions. The kind picker now has Runbook: for a procedure (a deploy, an alarm, an onboarding) the questions are the ones a runbook must answer, in order, with a checklist under what must be true first and numbered steps under the steps.
  • Easier to read. The Markdown marks, link addresses and small labels are a shade firmer in every theme, focus mode fades the other paragraphs less, buttons on the dark themes have readable labels, the keyboard's place shows in the Index and the contents, and the interface follows your Mac's Text Size setting (System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display). Increase Contrast firms everything up further.
  • Faster. Typing restyles only the paragraph you’re in, so a long document, or one with a Writing Replay record, keeps up with you. The contents scroll smoothly on a project of thousands of documents, and a folder of hundreds opens in half the time.
  • Also. The Index’s caption no longer lets the cards behind it show through; Go ▸ Today finds today’s entry wherever you’ve moved it instead of making a second one; and the margin’s first tab is Page.
  • The free trial starts by itself. Nothing to buy or sign into first: your seven days begin the day you get Grasmere, and the window just tells you so. (A trial started in 1.0 or 1.1 carries on as it was.)

1.1 · 20 October 2026

  • Convert Wiki-Links. Project ▸ Convert Wiki-Links rewrites every [[wiki-link]] in a project as a standard Markdown link, for documents brought from Obsidian: aliases, headings and embedded pictures are kept, links to documents that aren’t there can point to new documents made when followed, and the whole conversion can be undone. See Links and Backlinks and Support.
  • Writing Replay (experimental). A record of how a document is written, played back and exportable as a web page, with trusted timestamps. Off until turned on in Settings ▸ General. See Writing Replay.
  • New Document with Questions. File ▸ New Document with Questions has the AI model write Socratic questions about a subject you give, as the headings of a new document for you to answer in your own words. See Ask Your Documents and AI.
  • Ask a Follow-up Question. Edit ▸ Ask a Follow-up Question (⌥⌘/) has the model read the passage you’re writing and ask one question back, in the margin; add it as a heading or dismiss it.
  • Links to documents that don’t exist yet make them when followed, titled from the name, as wiki-links do.
  • Export as Word Document (⇧⌘E): the open document as a .docx with Word’s own styles, pictures embedded and links that work.
  • Markdown files on your Mac: Quick Look (Space in Finder) shows them in Grasmere’s style, and Grasmere can be the app that opens them (Settings ▸ General ▸ Markdown files), opening each document in its project, or on its own in a window of its own, with its links working.
  • Shortcuts as other writing apps have them: ⌘O opens a document by name (⌥⌘O for a file), ⌘P exports the page as a PDF, ⌥⌘1–3 make a heading and ⌥⌘Q a blockquote (several lines pasted into a quote are all quoted).
  • Standard buttons on the sheets, in the theme’s colours, and no trailing ellipses on menu items. The margin’s History section is called Git History. Lines are set at each font’s own height, with no spacing added by the app, so an empty line is as tall as a typed one and typing on it no longer moves the page.

1.0 · October 2026

The first release: documents as plain Markdown files, links that stay correct when documents are renamed or moved, the margin of backlinks and mentions, the Index, tasks and due dates, journal entries and quick capture, word targets, focus mode and split view, publishing to PDF, a website or Anki, and Ask Your Documents with Apple’s on-device model.
