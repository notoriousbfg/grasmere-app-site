Publishing

Your documents are plain files, so they already work anywhere. When you want something more finished, Grasmere can typeset them as a PDF or a Word document, build a website or make flash cards.

New in 1.4. Publishing happens on the Publish stage. Whatever you ask to publish, a document or a folder, goes in hand there, and you read it through and choose how it’s published before anything is written.

The Publish stage

New in 1.4.

Press ⌃4, choose Go ▸ Publish, or click PUBLISH in the strip at the top of the window. The stage works on what’s in hand: the documents you’ve tracked for review and publishing, the same ones the Review stage shows. See Review for tracking. Nothing in hand yet? The stage says so, with a Go to Review button, and tells you how to track something: right-click a document in the contents, or press ⌃T in the editor.

The stage has three columns.

  • In hand, on the left, lists the documents in the order they’ll be published. Each row has a status dot (hollow until the document has a status), its title, its words, and its synopsis beneath in italics. A document with review comments or changes still open says “3 open”; click that to deal with them on the Review screen. Drag a row up or down to change the order, which is kept in project.md’s In Hand section, with Undo in the notice. Click a row to see that document on the page, and double-click it, or press Return, to open it in the editor. Right-click for Open in Editor, Move Up, Move Down and Untrack. ⇧-click or ⌘-click to pick several: right-click ▸ Untrack 3 Documents, or ⌫, takes them all out of hand, with one Undo.
  • The page, in the middle, is everything in hand read straight through in your theme’s type, under a title page that follows the options as you change them. When what’s in hand comes from more than one folder, the page groups the documents into parts under their folders’ titles. Double-click a passage to open its document in the editor at that paragraph. The foot of the page has Read | Pages: Pages lays it all out as pages of the chosen style, Manuscript’s 12 pt Times double-spaced with your name, the title and the page number top right, or Book’s with the page number at the foot, footnotes on their page and the references after the last chapter. It’s a rough preview made in the window, not the PDF, which may break differently; see The Manuscript View.
  • The options, on the right: the Title (the folder’s name when everything in hand comes from one folder, otherwise the project’s, until you type another), the Author (optional), the style, Manuscript or Book, two Include boxes, and, when the project has sources, Citations, the style they’re written in. Choosing a citation style here sets it for the project, in project.md, and the page follows. Then Publish PDF, with Word beneath it. The options are kept while the window is open, and each window has its own.

The title bar says what’s there and how long it will be, “4 in hand · 1,344 words · about 6 pages”, and has Publish PDF too. The pages are an estimate, about 250 words a page in Manuscript style and 350 in Book style, until you switch to Pages, when it’s the count laid out; the PDF decides. The foot of the page names the style, and the citation style when there is one.

Readiness. Beneath the list, two lines check what’s in hand. The first is about links: “No broken links”, or how many lead nowhere, and in a project with sources how many are cited and whether every citation resolves (“1 citation doesn’t resolve” means a link written like a citation leads to a research note that isn’t a source). The second is about status: “2 chapters still in draft · 7 review items open”, or “Every chapter final · nothing open for review”. A ✓ means all’s well; a ○ means something to look at. You can publish anyway. To leave a document out, untrack it.

Draft. When every document in hand has a status and one of them isn’t Final, the title page says draft. Give a document its status on the Review stage’s cards, or as status: in its front matter. See The Manuscript View.

Publish a folder to PDF

To turn a folder of chapters into one PDF, right-click the folder in the contents and choose Publish to PDF. You can also use the File menu, which offers Publish Project to PDF, or Publish “chapters” to PDF when you’ve highlighted a folder in the contents with the arrow keys. Open as Manuscript does the same. See The Manuscript View.

New in 1.4. Either way, the folder’s documents go in hand, in the folder’s order, and the Publish stage opens. Anything already in hand stays. There’s no sheet any more: the title, author and style are the options on the stage, and Publish PDF asks where to save.

Grasmere typesets the documents in the order they’re in hand. Each document becomes a chapter on a new page, titled by its first heading, and each folder inside becomes a part.

Give the PDF a title, and an author if you like, then choose a style:

  • Manuscript: standard manuscript format, as publishers and agents ask for. Double-spaced 12 pt Times, one-inch margins, a title page with the word count, and the author, title and page number at the top of each page. No contents page: a manuscript by convention has none.
  • Book: your theme’s typography, large chapter titles, a divider page for each part, a contents page with the page number of each part and chapter (new in 1.4), and page numbers at the foot of every page.

Include. New in 1.4. Two boxes, both ticked to begin with. Title page puts the title, the author and the word count on a page of their own before the first chapter; untick it and the PDF (or Word file) starts on the first chapter, with that page numbered 1. Folder titles gives each folder the documents in hand span a page of its own, as a part of the book (in Book style; a manuscript carries straight on); untick it and the chapters follow one another. The page in the middle follows as you tick and untick. A single document in hand with both boxes off comes out as a PDF of that document and nothing more.

The folder’s order comes from its index.md. See The Contents. When the PDF is saved, Show in Finder takes you to it.

New in 1.4. PDFs are typeset by Paged.js, so they have what a typeset book has. Preview’s sidebar lists the parts, chapters and their headings as bookmarks. A link to a heading, to another chapter or to the web works when you click it. Footnotes sit at the foot of the page they’re referred to on, numbered from 1 in each chapter. See Writing and Formatting.

Export a document as a PDF

File ▸ Export ▸ PDF (⌘P) typesets the open document on A4 or Letter pages.

New in 1.4. In a project, the document goes in hand, joining whatever is there, and the Publish stage opens; press Publish PDF when you’re ready. A document opened on its own, outside a project, goes straight to the save panel.

Export a document as a Word document

New in 1.1.

When you need to hand something in, or send it to someone who edits in Word, choose File ▸ Export ▸ Word Document (⇧⌘E). Grasmere saves the document as a .docx on A4 or Letter pages, with page numbers at the foot.

New in 1.4. In a project, this goes to the Publish stage like a PDF, and Word, beneath Publish PDF, writes what’s in hand as one plain Word document: a title page in Word’s Title style, each part and chapter from a new page under a Heading 1, citations in the project’s style with a bibliography last, real footnotes, and pictures embedded. Nothing is kept for a review. When you want a copy to send to a reviewer and bring back, use the Review stage’s Send instead. See Review. A document opened on its own goes straight to the save panel.

It’s a proper Word document, not a picture of one. Headings use Word’s Heading 1, 2 and 3, so they fill the navigation pane and a table of contents. Lists, tables, quotations and code use Word’s own styles too, so if you change the style set in Word, the whole document follows. Pictures are embedded and web links can be clicked.

Links to other documents become plain text. A link to a heading in the same document still jumps to it.

Build a website

Project ▸ Build Website (⌥⌘B) turns your project into a website, in a _site folder inside it. Each document gets a page, with working links, its pictures, and a list of the documents that link to it.

The home page shows the project’s name, the Summary section of project.md, and the contents. Pages are styled with your light and dark themes. There’s no JavaScript and nothing that tracks visitors.

A few things worth knowing:

  • To leave a document out, put draft: true or publish: false in its front matter. Links to it become plain text.
  • Linked files such as PDFs, and the research folder’s saved web pages and insights, are often other people’s work, so they’re left out unless you include them in Settings ▸ Automation.
  • Before the first build, Grasmere shows you what will be published. After that, a rebuild only changes what has changed, and never touches files Grasmere didn’t write.
  • Footnotes (new in 1.4) become numbered notes at the end of the page, each number linking down to its note and the note linking back.

When it’s done, Open in Browser shows you the site.

Publish

Project ▸ Publish (⇧⌘P) does the whole round in one go. It saves your documents, builds the website if you’ve asked it to and commits anything waiting to git. Then it runs a Shortcut of your choosing with the project folder, to upload the site or push it, say. Choose the Shortcut in Settings ▸ Automation ▸ Publish with.

Export to Anki

Grasmere can turn documents into flash cards for Anki, with the document’s title on the front and the document on the back.

  • File ▸ Export ▸ Anki Deck exports the open document, straight away.
  • Right-click a folder and choose Export Folder to Anki for every document in it.
  • Pick several documents in the contents, right-click, and choose Export Documents to Anki.

If you import a later export, Anki updates the cards and keeps your reviews.
