Review

New in 1.4. The Review screen is where a draft and its reviewers meet. You send a document, or a whole book, as a Word file. The reviewer comments and suggests changes in whatever they use, and when their copy comes back, Grasmere marks their suggestions and comments in the page itself, their words struck through and underlined in their colour. You accept, reject, defer or resolve them one at a time, reply to their comments, and send the next round. Nothing goes into your Markdown until you say so.

Each sending and coming back is a round. Grasmere keeps the rounds, the copies sent and every comment and change beside the document, in its review file, never in your text.

The Review screen

Choose File ▸ Review (⇧⌘R) with a document open, and the window fills with the document as one page you can write in. Highlight a folder in the contents first and ⇧⌘R opens its chapters as one continuous page, a book to read and review straight through. The margin’s Review section has Open Review at its top, and on the Review stage’s board (see below) a double click on a card opens its document here.

The title bar keeps the stage strip, with ‹ All in Review at its right to go back to the board. The row beneath has the document’s name (a menu of everything in hand, to switch to another), Compare with, the word count, Send ▾ and Items.

On the left:

  • Versions lists Now and then, newest first, every version Grasmere knows: the review rounds, the copies sent, the restore points (Before the review, Before restoring) and, in a git repository, the commits that changed the document, with their author and short hash (the newest sixty).
  • Chapters, for a book, lists the chapters with their words and how many review items each has open (“3 open”). Click one and the page goes to it. As you scroll, the mark follows the chapter you’re reading.

Pick a version, there or in the header’s Compare with menu, and a strip above the page names it, with its date and the difference in words (“312 more words than then”). The page then shows what’s changed since: words added since underlined, words taken out struck through where they were, a paragraph taken out struck on its own lines. Point at a taken-out passage and Restore this passage puts it back as one edit, so ⌘Z takes it out again. For a commit of a single document, Restore This Version in the strip brings the whole version back, again as one step you can undo. The × in the strip, or Now, stops comparing. A review’s own version holds only the copy sent and the changes, so it has no word count to compare.

Escape, ⌘W, the window’s close button or File ▸ Close Review (⇧⌘R again) close the screen, every chapter saved. If the find box is open, the first Escape closes that.

While you’re on the page, the File, Edit, Format, Insert and View commands that act on text act on the chapter that has the cursor. Commands that open something in the window behind it, such as New Document or the Go menu, are dimmed until you close the screen.

Sending it

Choose Send ▾ ▸ Export as Word in the Review screen’s header. The save panel offers “Start Here (Round 1).docx”, with the project’s citation style if the document cites sources, and the export starts the round. You aren’t asked for anyone’s name: send the file to whoever you like, as you would any other. Reviewers can use:

  • Microsoft Word. Comments, replies and Track Changes all come back.
  • Pages. Comments and tracked changes come back if they export to Word with File ▸ Export To ▸ Word.
  • Google Docs. They upload the file, comment and use Suggesting mode, then choose File ▸ Download ▸ Microsoft Word. Suggestions come back as tracked changes.

Each paragraph in the file carries a hidden marker so Grasmere can tell which paragraph is which when it comes back. It’s a Word bookmark whose name starts with an underscore, which Word leaves out of its Bookmarks list. Reviewers won’t see it and don’t have to do anything with it. Word keeps the markers. Nobody says whether Pages and Google Docs do, but it hardly matters, because Grasmere matches paragraphs by their words when the markers are gone. The file also carries a custom document property, Grasmere sent copy, that names the round it belongs to.

Grasmere keeps a copy of exactly what it sent. That way it can tell what the reviewer changed, even if you’ve carried on writing since.

The Word file holds more than the text. Your own comments (see below), and any reviewer’s comment you’ve replied to with its whole thread, go out as real Word comments, so the conversation carries on in Word. A thread you’ve resolved since the last copy went out arrives marked done. Footnotes are real Word footnotes, numbered from 1 in each chapter of a book. Citations are formatted in the chosen style, with a bibliography at the end.

Export with My Changes Marked, beneath Export as Word, sends the next round with every change you’ve made since the copy last sent shown as a tracked change in your name, so a reviewer sees what you did with their suggestions. It’s greyed, with “Export as Word first”, until a copy has been sent.

Until the copy comes back, a bar beneath the header says so: “Round 1 sent 26 Oct, 13:20 as “Start Here (Round 1).docx” · waiting for it to come back”, with Import Returned File and Show in Finder. The same line sits at the top of the margin’s Review section in the editor, with Waiting in the section’s header, so you can keep writing and still see where things stand.

Word from the Publish stage is a different thing: a plain Word document of everything in hand, for handing in or printing, which starts no round. The reviewer’s copy is the Review screen’s Send, or the Review stage’s Send for Review. In a project, File ▸ Export ▸ Word Document (⇧⌘E) opens the Publish stage with the document in hand, so it makes that plain copy too.

Bringing it back

When the reviewer’s file arrives, choose Import Returned File in the waiting bar or the margin’s line, or drop the .docx on the Review screen or on the editor while a round is waiting. File ▸ Import Review works as well, with the document open.

Grasmere works out which round the file belongs to: by the copy’s id if the file still carries it, else by the text as it was sent, else by the round whose text is closest. Only if two waiting rounds are about as close does it ask which, by when each was sent. The same copy imported twice is recognised (“You’ve already imported this copy”), so nothing is added again, even if the file was renamed. A reviewer’s reply to one of your comments joins its thread, and the notice says so (“1 reply joined its comment”); your own comments coming back aren’t added twice.

In Versions the round’s row changes from “Round 1 · sent 26 Oct, 13:20” to “Round 1 · back from Rosa”, with Sent › Back › Done beneath it and a tally of where its items stand (“3 accepted, 1 rejected, 2 deferred”). Done lights once everyone is back and nothing is open. A copy with nobody’s name in it, say one edited without tracking, reads “Round 1 · back 26 Oct, 13:24”. Pick the row and it shows each copy that came back, who from and when, with Import Returned File for another reviewer’s copy of the same round. A second reviewer’s file joins the round the same way (“back from Rosa and Sam”).

The screen opens on the newest round with open items, and marks only that round’s. Pick another round in Versions to see its items instead. Copies sent and reviews imported before 1.4 show as rounds of their own, with nothing lost.

Reading the review on the page

Their changes are in the page where they made them. A word they took out is struck through, a word they added is underlined after it, each in the reviewer’s colour. A paragraph taken out is struck on its own lines; a paragraph added is shown as a block. A comment washes its passage faintly, with a balloon beside the page. None of it is in your text: drag over an underlined word that isn’t yours and the cursor goes round it, copy a paragraph and you get the file’s words, and the file on disk is unchanged until you accept.

Changes made with Track Changes carry the reviewer’s name and the date. If the reviewer changed something without tracking it, Grasmere still finds it by comparing their copy with the one it sent, and marks it not tracked. A paragraph the reviewer added or took out counts as one item.

Point at a change, or put the cursor in it, and a small pill offers Accept, Reject and Defer; a comment’s offers Resolve and Defer. From the keyboard, File ▸ Review Changes has Next Change (⌃⌘↓) and Previous Change (⌃⌘↑), which go mark to mark and on into the next chapter of a book, and Accept Change at Cursor (⌃⌘A), Reject Change at Cursor (⌃⌘R) and Defer Change at Cursor for the one the cursor is in.

A chapter’s front matter is folded to one faint line naming its fields (“synopsis, status”). Click it, or move the cursor into it, and it opens for editing. Only on this page: a chapter opened in the editor shows its front matter as before.

Two comments close together push the second balloon down below the first. In a narrow window, or with the items panel showing, the balloons give way and the comments are in the panel.

The items panel

Items in the header shows and hides a panel on the right with every item of the round as a card: the comment or the change, who from and when, with the same Accept, Reject, Defer, Resolve and Reply. The header counts what’s open (“8 open”), and each reviewer has a line with a dot in their colour, their count, and Accept All and Reject All. For a book the items are grouped under each chapter’s name with its count. Click a card and the page goes to the passage with the cursor in it.

Accept All and Reject All deal with all that reviewer’s changes at once. Their comments stay. Accept All is one step for Undo, one per chapter in a book.

Accepting, rejecting, resolving, deferring

  • Accept puts the change into your text. It’s an ordinary edit, so ⌘Z takes it back (the item stays accepted).
  • Reject leaves your text as it is.
  • Resolve marks a comment as dealt with.
  • Reply adds your reply under a reviewer’s comment and keeps it open. Reply and Resolve does both.
  • Defer leaves an item for the next round. It comes off the open count and is listed under “N deferred” in the items panel, with Open Again. When the next round is sent, every deferred item opens again and belongs to that round. Defer is on the pill, the items panel’s cards and their right-click menu, the margin’s line and its right-click menu, and File ▸ Review Changes. You can’t defer your own comments.

Comments read as threads. Each message has a circle of initials, the name in bold (“You” in your colour, each reviewer in theirs) and the time, with replies beneath, oldest first. Resolve is at the top right; ⋯ has Defer on theirs, or Edit and Delete on yours. The Reply field at the bottom grows as you type and shows Cancel and Reply.

You can keep writing while a document is out for review. If you accept a change in a paragraph you’ve edited since, Grasmere fits the reviewer’s change into your new version. Say you rewrote the start of a sentence and they changed a word at the end. Both changes stand.

It’s only a real conflict when you both changed the same words. Then the card in the items panel shows three versions, one above the other:

  • Sent is what they saw, with their words struck through.
  • Suggested is their version, with their words underlined.
  • Now is yours, with the words you changed picked out.

If you want their change, make it by hand and then reject the item. A change that would cut through a link or bold text can’t be accepted either, so a link is never left half-written. The card tells you when this happens.

With VoiceOver, each item reads as one sentence saying who, when and what. Its Accept, Reject, Defer and Resolve are in the Actions rotor, and on the page the pill reads “Accept, button” and “Reject, button”.

Your own comments

You can comment on your own draft, as you would in Word, to leave yourself a note or to ask the reviewer something. Select a passage, or put the cursor in a word, and choose Insert ▸ Comment (⌥⌘A) or right-click ▸ Comment. It works in the editor and on the Review screen, not in the side pane, and not on front matter. A small field opens under the passage with your name in your colour and the words quoted. Type, then press Return or click Add Comment; Escape or Cancel leaves without adding.

The comment is kept beside the document in its review file, never in the text. It shows as “Your comment” in your colour, in a balloon on the Review screen, as a card in the items panel and as a line in the margin, each with Edit, Delete and Resolve (in the margin, right-click the line for Edit Comment and Delete Comment). Your name is the one you commit with, if the project is a git repository, or your account’s name.

When you send the next round, your open comments go out as Word comments in your name, and a reviewer can reply to them. Their reply joins your comment’s thread when the file comes back.

The Review stage

The Review screen looks after one document or one book. The Review stage, ⌃3 or REVIEW in the strip, is the board for everything you’re working towards: the documents in hand.

On the left is the tray, IN HAND: the documents in hand, numbered, each with its status dot and how many review items it has open. Drag a row to reorder, click its × to untrack it, or use Untrack All. Track more documents slides the contents in beside the tray (⌃⌘S does the same) so you can drag documents onto it or right-click one ▸ Track; Publish › goes on to the Publish stage. In the middle are the documents as index cards, grouped by folder, with title, synopsis and words; double-click a card, or pick it with the arrow keys and press Return, to open it on the Review screen, and right-click it to set its status. On the right, OPEN ITEMS lists every open comment and change across what’s in hand, newest round first, four a document and then “N more · open the document ›”. Accept there puts the change into the document without opening it, with Undo in the notice; Reject, Resolve, Defer, Reply, Edit and Delete work as on the Review screen.

There are three ways to put a document in hand: right-click it, or a folder, in the contents and choose Track (a folder tracks everything in it, in order); drag it from the contents onto the tray; or, in the editor, the margin’s Track for Review (⌃T) at the top of its Page tab, which reads In hand with Untrack once it is. With nothing in hand the stage explains the three ways and offers to track the chapters folder. Tracking writes one line to an In Hand section of project.md, so the tray is in your files, and the notice offers Undo.

The title bar’s right reads “In hand: 4 · 1,341 words” and has Send for Review ▾: Export as Word sends everything in hand as one Word document and one round (“chapters (Round 1).docx”; one folder’s documents make that folder’s book round, documents from several folders keep their round beside the project’s top); Import Returned File brings the copy back into it; Restore to Before the Review is the way back. A line above the cards says when a round is waiting, with Import Returned File and Show in Finder, and a .docx dropped on the board imports it.

The strip’s REVIEW carries a badge with the open comments and changes across everything in hand. It drops as you accept, reject, resolve or defer.

A whole book

A folder’s chapters are reviewed as one. Highlight the folder in the contents and choose File ▸ Review, or track it and open a chapter from the board. Send ▾ ▸ Export as Word writes one Word file for every chapter, with Grasmere noting which chapter each paragraph came from, and the file is named for the folder (“chapters (Round 1).docx”).

When the copy comes back, the whole book is compared at once, so it still works if the markers are lost. Each comment and change goes to its own chapter, marked on the page in that chapter, and Chapters shows how many each has left to deal with (“3 open”). Open a chapter in the editor and its items are in the margin, as with a single document.

If you’ve moved a passage to another chapter since, its comments and changes follow it there. Some things don’t belong to any one chapter, like a comment on the title page, a part’s title or the references. Those, and anything on a chapter you’ve since deleted, go to Review notes, a document in the book’s folder. Each gets a task line with the reviewer, the date, what they said and what it was on, so they show in Tasks and you can tick them off.

Review notes is an ordinary document, but its front matter says review: notes, so the Publish stage and Export as Word leave it out of the book. Importing the same copy twice adds nothing to it.

Going back

Every time a review comes in, Grasmere first keeps a copy of each document it covers, as it is at that moment. For a single document that’s the one document. For a book it’s every chapter, plus its Review notes. These are the Before the review restore points in Versions.

If you change your mind about what you accepted, choose Restore to Before the Review from the Review screen’s Send ▾ menu, the File menu, the margin’s ⋯ menu or the board’s Send for Review ▾. It lists the documents that have changed since the review came in and puts them back as they were. For a book it restores them all at once. The changes you’d accepted show on the page again.

Restoring the open document is an ordinary edit, so ⌘Z takes it back. Grasmere also keeps what the documents said just before you restored (Before restoring in Versions), so choosing the command again reverses the restore.

The copies are kept in the hidden .review folder. Only the documents a review can change are copied, never the whole project, and each folder keeps the ten most recent.

What’s ignored

Only words count. Changes to fonts, styles, spacing, colours, page size or layout are never shown and never applied. A reviewer can reformat the whole document and you’ll only see what they changed in the text. Headers, footers, text boxes and pictures aren’t compared either.

Unplaced items

Sometimes an item’s passage isn’t in the document any more, say because you deleted that paragraph. The item goes to Unplaced at the end of the items panel and the margin’s section, with the text it was about, so nothing is dropped. Resolve it once you’ve read it.

The margin

In the editor, the margin’s Page tab starts with a Review section while a review is under way: who it’s from, Open Review, and one line an item in document order, the comment or the words struck and underlined, with Accept and Reject or Resolve, and Defer. Click a line and the passage is washed on the page. The header counts what’s open (“3 open”), or says All dealt with or Waiting. Replies, Accept All and the conversation are on the Review screen. See The Margin.

Where review files live

Each document’s review is kept in a small file beside it, in a hidden .review folder. Chapter One.md has .review/Chapter One.md.json. The file holds the rounds, the copies sent, the reviews imported, and every comment and change, open or dealt with, yours included. It’s plain JSON, so any text editor can read it.

A few things worth knowing:

  • The review file goes wherever the folder goes, and Grasmere moves it when you rename or move the document in the app.
  • If you copy a document on its own in the Finder, its review stays behind in the .review folder where it was.
  • To find the file, choose Show Review File in Finder from the margin’s Review section’s ⋯ menu. Press ⇧⌘. in a Finder window to see hidden folders.
  • For a book, the rounds, the record of which chapter each paragraph came from, and the restore copies, are in the .review folder of the book’s own folder.
  • Like every hidden folder, it’s left out of the contents, links, search and your website.
  • Git ignores it. The folder has its own .gitignore, and Grasmere’s lines in your project’s .gitignore list it too.

Your Markdown never holds review marks. Comments wait in the review file until you deal with them, and accepted changes go into the text as ordinary writing.
