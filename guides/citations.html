Citations

New in 1.3. Cite the books, papers and web pages you’ve read, and Grasmere writes the citations and the reference list for you when you export, in APA, Harvard, Chicago or MLA. There’s nothing to learn: a source is a document in your research folder, and a citation is an ordinary link to it.

Sources

A source is any document in the research folder (research, unless you’ve named another in Settings ▸ General ▸ Research) whose front matter has a type: webpage, book, article, chapter, report or other. Every page you keep with File ▸ Save Web Page is one already.

You can tell a source at a glance: in the contents it has an icon for what it is (a book, a globe for a web page, a page for an article), and at the top of the margin’s Page tab it says “A source · Book”. Any other document in the research folder says it’s a research note, with a Make It a Source menu that writes the type for you. Its fields are the ones a reference needs:

---
title: The Living Loaf
author:
  - Field, Rosa
  - Tom Baker
published: 2019
publisher: Riverbank Press
place: London
edition: 2
type: book
---
  • Everyone: title, author (one name, or a list) and published (a date, a month or just a year).
  • A web page: site, url and accessed.
  • A book: publisher, place, edition and isbn.
  • An article: container (the journal), volume, issue, pages and doi.
  • A chapter: container (the book), pages, publisher and place.

Write names either way round, “Field, Rosa” or “Rosa Field”; several on one line work too (“Rosa Field and Tom Baker”). A source with no date is cited “n.d.”. Edit the fields in the margin’s Front Matter section, and write your own notes on the source below them: they’re yours, and never exported.

New Source

File ▸ New Source makes a source for you:

  • A DOI (10.1038/nature12373, or its doi.org address): a paper’s details are looked up at doi.org.
  • An ISBN: a book’s details are looked up at Open Library.
  • A web address: Save Web Page takes over and saves the page.
  • Blank Source: a book (or article, chapter, report, web page) with its fields empty, to fill in by hand.

The new document opens, named after its title. A DOI or ISBN you’ve saved before isn’t saved twice: Grasmere offers the document you already have. The lookups happen only when you ask, and send only the DOI or ISBN.

Citing

Type [@ where the citation goes, or choose Edit ▸ Cite Source (⌥⌘K). A list of your sources appears; type part of an author’s name, a word of the title or a year to narrow it, then press Return. Grasmere writes a link to the source:

Starters are alive [Field 2019](../research/The%20Living%20Loaf.md).

To cite a page, add it after a comma inside the brackets: [Field 2019, p. 12](…). Pages, ranges (pp. 12–14), chapters (ch. 3), paragraphs (para. 4) and sections are kept, in the style’s own form.

Because a citation is a link, everything links do, citations do: they follow the source when it’s renamed or moved, show a preview on hover, and list the citing documents under Linked From in the source’s margin. In the editor it stays a link.

Inside your own brackets, a citation leaves them be, so (see [Field 2019](…), p. 4; [Abbott 2021](…)) becomes “(see Field, 2019, p. 4; Abbott, 2021)”.

Styles

When you export (Export as PDF, Export as Word Document, Publish to PDF, the Manuscript view’s exports and Build Website), each citation is written in the project’s style and the sources you cited are listed at the end, in the style’s order:

StyleIn the textThe book in the list
APA 7(Field, 2019, p. 12)Field, R. (2019). The Living Loaf (2nd ed.). Riverbank Press.
Harvard(Field, 2019, p. 12)Field, R. (2019) The Living Loaf. 2nd edn. London: Riverbank Press.
Chicago author-date(Field 2019, 12)Field, Rosa. 2019. The Living Loaf. 2nd ed. London: Riverbank Press.
MLA 9(Field 12)Field, Rosa. The Living Loaf. 2nd ed., Riverbank Press, 2019.

The list is headed References (Works Cited in MLA). Two works by the same authors in one year get letters (2019a, 2019b); in MLA, an author with two works cited has the title in the citation. On a website, each citation links to its entry. Choose None to keep citations as links.

The style is chosen in the export’s save panel or sheet (APA unless you choose another) and kept in project.md, where you can change it by hand:

## Citations

Style: Chicago

Titles are printed as you wrote them, so write them in the capitalisation your style asks for (APA wants only the first word and proper nouns capitalised in a book’s or article’s title).

research.md

Grasmere keeps a table of your sources in the research folder, research.md: each source’s title (linking to it), author, year, type, site or publisher, the day you accessed it, and how many documents cite it. It’s brought up to date whenever a source or a citation changes. Write anything you like above or below the table; the table itself is Grasmere’s, and changes to it are replaced. If you’d rather not see it, turn off Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Show research.md in the contents and Manuscripts: it’s still kept up to date, but it’s left out of the contents, out of a Manuscript of the research folder, and out of each source’s Linked from.

Checking

With Settings ▸ General ▸ Check the Markdown as you write on, a link that reads like a citation (“Field 2019”) to a document that isn’t a source is pointed out in the margin’s Problems section, so you can give it a type.

What isn’t supported

  • Footnote styles (Chicago notes and bibliography, OSCOLA), numbered styles (Vancouver, IEEE) and journals’ house styles.
  • Citing an editor rather than an author, translators, and narrative citations (“Field (2019) argues”): write the name in your sentence and cite the page.
  • Zotero, BibTeX, CSL files and other reference managers: sources are documents in your project.
